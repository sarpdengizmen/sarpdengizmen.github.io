Desk Lamp

This desk lamp started as a simple question: what if a lamp wasn't just a functional object but something you shaped to fit your space — and your mood? The result is a modular lighting structure built almost entirely from 3D-printed parts, where the user defines the form by arranging the light-bearing beams however they choose.

The concept draws from topographic maps — the idea that a terrain can be described by layered contours, each telling part of a larger story. Here, the beams are the contours: stack them tightly and the lamp feels dense and sculptural; spread them wide and it becomes airy and minimal. No configuration is wrong.

Material Engineering: Translucent PETG

The beams are printed in translucent PETG, a material chosen specifically for its light-transmitting properties. But raw PETG transmits light in an uneven, blotchy way. The key insight was that print orientation and layer height dramatically affect the optical result — finer layers scatter light more evenly, while specific infill patterns create internal refraction that softens and diffuses the output. Several print sequences were tested before landing on one that produces a consistent, warm glow along the full length of each beam.

The base and mounting hardware are printed in matte black PLA, disappearing into the background and keeping the eye on the light. The only non-printed components are the LED strip, power supply, and wiring — everything structural is printed.

WLED: Open-Source Intelligence

The lamp runs on WLED, an open-source firmware for addressable LED strips. An ESP8266/ESP32 microcontroller hosts a local web server and can connect to a home network, giving full control through any browser or the WLED mobile app. From there, the lamp supports hundreds of built-in effects — from solid static colour to reactive audio visualisation — and the user can save custom presets or schedule automations.

This makes the lamp genuinely "living": it changes character throughout the day. A slow, warm amber in the evening is a few taps away from a crisp white for focused work, or a cycling colour palette for ambiance. The open ecosystem also means the lamp can be integrated into home automation setups like Home Assistant with no additional hardware.

Assembly

Beams slot into the base via dovetail joints — no tools needed. They can be added, removed, or repositioned in seconds. The LED strip runs inside a channel in the base and feeds up through the beam mounting points. The whole lamp assembles in under ten minutes from a cold print.
