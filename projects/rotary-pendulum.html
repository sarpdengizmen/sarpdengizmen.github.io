Rotary Inverted Pendulum

The rotary inverted pendulum is a canonical benchmark in control theory — a fundamentally unstable system that must be actively balanced at its upright equilibrium. This project covers the full control engineering pipeline: mathematical modelling, controller design, state estimation, and hardware validation on a physical rig.

The system consists of a motorised rotating arm driving a freely-pivoting pendulum rod. Left uncontrolled, the pendulum falls immediately. The goal is to drive it to the upright position from rest and hold it there indefinitely in the face of real-world disturbances — friction, sensor noise, and model uncertainty.

System Modelling

The equations of motion were derived from Lagrangian mechanics, yielding a coupled nonlinear ODE system in the arm angle and pendulum angle. These were linearised around the unstable upright equilibrium to produce a state-space representation suitable for linear control design. The linearised model was validated against physical measurements and refined for parameter accuracy (motor torque constant, friction coefficients, moments of inertia).

Swing-Up Strategy

Getting the pendulum from its natural hanging position to the upright requires a nonlinear strategy — linear controllers cannot handle the full range of motion. An energy-based swing-up controller was implemented that pumps energy into the pendulum by modulating the arm velocity in sync with the pendulum's natural oscillation. Once the pendulum reaches near-vertical (within a defined catch window), authority is handed off to the balancing controller. The handoff timing is critical: too early and the linear controller saturates; too late and the pendulum overshoots the catch window.

Balancing Controller

At the upright equilibrium, a pole-placement controller was designed to assign the closed-loop eigenvalues to target locations in the left-half complex plane. An optimal variant using LQR (Linear Quadratic Regulator) was also implemented, trading off state regulation energy against control effort through the Q and R weighting matrices. The LQR controller was found to give better disturbance rejection with less aggressive control action than the pole-placement design.

State Estimation — Kalman Filter

The physical rig provides direct measurements of arm angle and pendulum angle (via encoders), but the controller requires angular velocities as well. Numerical differentiation of encoder signals amplifies quantisation noise, making full-state feedback from raw sensors impractical. A discrete Kalman filter was designed to fuse the noisy encoder measurements into optimal estimates of the full state vector — position and velocity for both degrees of freedom. The filter covariance matrices were tuned to balance responsiveness against noise rejection.
