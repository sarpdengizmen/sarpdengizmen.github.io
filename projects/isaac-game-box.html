Isaac Game Box

"The Binding of Isaac: Four Souls" is a card game that ships in a modest box with loose components that inevitably scatter across a table — cards mixed with tokens, dice lost in corners, rulebooks dog-eared and forgotten. This project is a purpose-built solution: an all-in-one storage container that converts into a fully functional game organiser for every component in the set.

The design constraint was that the box needed to serve two masters: as a closed container it should look like an intentional object, something you'd want on your shelf. Open, it becomes a game table — cards organised in accessible fans, tokens sorted by type, dice in dedicated cups. The transformation is part of the experience.

Structure and Mechanism

The box uses a hinged lid that locks flat when closed, and props at 90° when open to form the back wall of the play area. Internal dividers are press-fit and removable, allowing the layout to adapt to game expansions. Card slots are sized to exactly fit sleeved cards — a critical detail for players who protect their collections.

The base is 3D printed in PLA with a wood-fill filament for the structural parts, giving the surface a natural grain texture that complements the laser-engraved panels. All joints are chamfered to prevent card corners from catching during setup and teardown.

Laser-Engraved Wood Panels

The exterior panels are thin plywood sheets laser-engraved with iconography drawn directly from the game — characters, symbols, and border motifs that match the hand-drawn, slightly grotesque aesthetic of the original artwork. The engravings were vectorised from reference images and cleaned up in Inkscape before being sent to the laser cutter.

The contrast between the dark engraved areas and the natural wood creates a high-impact visual at zero added paint. A matte finish seals the wood and prevents it from absorbing moisture over time.

Component Fit

Every compartment was prototyped against the physical components before final print. The token trays are the tightest tolerance — they hold components securely enough that the box can be transported upside down without anything shifting, but loose enough that tokens can be grabbed with one hand during play.
