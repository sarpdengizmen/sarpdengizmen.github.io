Vertical Axis Wind Turbine

This project is a Darrieus-type vertical axis wind turbine (VAWT) designed, built, and tested as part of the ME-407 Energy Systems course at METU. The design targets the urban and near-urban environment — where wind is turbulent, directionally variable, and often arriving at low speeds — conditions where horizontal-axis turbines struggle and VAWTs have a structural advantage.

Unlike a conventional propeller turbine that must yaw to face the wind, a VAWT accepts wind from any direction and works with the same efficiency regardless of approach angle. This omnidirectional characteristic makes VAWTs well-suited for rooftop installation, city edges, and any site with unpredictable wind roses.

Aerodynamic Design

The blade profile was selected through a literature survey of NACA aerofoil sections optimised for low tip-speed-ratio operation — the regime typical of urban wind. Blade geometry (chord length, pitch angle, aspect ratio) was sized using the Double Multiple Streamtube (DMST) model, which divides the rotor into upstream and downstream half-cycles and applies blade element theory to each separately. The DMST output was validated against published experimental data for similar configurations before finalising the geometry.

Blade count and solidity were traded off: higher solidity improves low-speed startup torque at the cost of peak efficiency. A three-blade configuration was chosen as the best balance for the target operating range.

Structural Design and Manufacturing

The blades were manufactured from laser-cut aluminium sheet, formed over a jig to maintain the aerofoil profile, and reinforced with internal ribs to resist torsional deformation under aerodynamic load. The central shaft and arm assembly are machined steel, selected for the fatigue resistance required in a continuously rotating system. All joints were designed with access in mind — the turbine can be fully disassembled in the lab without special tools.

Wind Tunnel Testing

The completed turbine was tested in METU's wind tunnel facility across a range of wind speeds. Power output (torque × angular velocity) was measured via a braking dynamometer, and the power coefficient Cp was calculated as a function of tip-speed ratio (TSR). The Cp–TSR curve was compared against the DMST predictions. Measured peak Cp fell within the expected range, with deviations attributed to strut drag and tip losses not captured in the simplified analytical model.
