Vertical Axis Wind Turbine

This project is a mixede-type vertical axis wind turbine (VAWT) designed, built, and tested as part of the ME-407 final year design course at METU. The design targets the urban and near-urban environment — where wind is turbulent, directionally variable, and often arriving at low speeds — conditions where horizontal-axis turbines struggle and VAWTs have a structural advantage.

Unlike a conventional propeller turbine that must yaw to face the wind, a VAWT accepts wind from any direction and works with the same efficiency regardless of approach angle. This omnidirectional characteristic makes VAWTs well-suited for rooftop installation, city edges, and any site with unpredictable wind roses.

Aerodynamic Design

The expected performance of the turbine was predicted using the Double-Multiple Streamtube (DMST) aerodynamic model, which divides the flow into multiple streamtubes and applies blade element theory to compute the forces on each blade element. The analysis was also validated with CFD simulations.

Structural Design and Analysis

The turbine's structural design was informed by the aerodynamic loading predictions. Finite element analysis (FEA) was performed to ensure the blades and shaft could withstand the expected stresses. The design was iterated to balance strength, weight, and manufacturability.

Manufacturing

The blades were manufactured from wire cut foam, and reinforced with internal ribs and spars to resist deformation under aerodynamic load. The central shaft and arm assembly are machined steel, selected for the fatigue resistance required in a continuously rotating system. All joints were designed with access in mind — the turbine can be fully disassembled in the lab without special tools.

Power Generation System and Safety

The turbine was coupled to a permanent magnet DC generator through a belt drive system, to achieve the necessary speed for efficient power generation. The electrical output was conditioned through a solar charge controller to regulate voltage and current, allowing for safe and controlled charging of a 12V lead-acid battery. The system was also instrumented with microcontroller controlled relay system that shorts the DC motor achieveing braking torque in the case of dangerously high rotational speeds.

Wind Tunnel Testing

The completed turbine was tested in METU's wind tunnel facility across a range of wind speeds.
