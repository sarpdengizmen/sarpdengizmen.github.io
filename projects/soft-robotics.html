Soft Robotics Gripper

Conventional rigid grippers struggle with delicate, irregular, or deformable objects — a fruit bruised by too much clamping force, a circuit board cracked by misaligned jaws. Soft robotics addresses this with actuators made from compliant materials that deform continuously under pneumatic pressure, conforming naturally to whatever they grasp. This project is a pneumatically actuated soft gripper designed, moulded, and tested from first principles.

The finger design is a bending actuator: an elastomeric body with an embedded strain-limiting layer along one face. When pressurised, the body wants to expand uniformly — but the inextensible layer on one side prevents elongation there, forcing the structure to curve. This predictable bending kinematics is the basis for most soft gripper fingers used in research and industry today.

Mould Design and Fabrication

The finger geometry was designed in CAD and the corresponding moulds were 3D printed in PLA. Ecoflex 00-30 silicone was mixed, degassed under vacuum, and cast in two stages: first the body with embedded channel geometry, then the strain-limiting backing layer using a stiffer silicone (Dragon Skin 10). The two-material approach gives the desired asymmetric stiffness without requiring a separate fabric inlay.

Post-cure, the fingers were demolded, trimmed, and bonded to a common pneumatic manifold that allows each finger to be addressed independently from a single pressure source. The casting process required iteration to eliminate air pockets at fine channel features — a problem resolved by adjusting the mould fill orientation and introducing degassing channels.

Gripper Assembly and Actuation

Three fingers are arranged symmetrically around a central hub, equally spaced at 120°. This configuration allows the gripper to grasp cylindrical objects in power grip and small objects in pinch grip simply by varying the actuation pressure — no mechanical reconfiguration required. The hub includes a standard robot flange interface for attachment to a manipulator or test rig.

Actuation is provided by a small air compressor regulated to a working pressure of 40–80 kPa. Solenoid valves control each finger independently under manual or programmatic control. The gripper's compliance means that gripping force scales with pressure and contact geometry rather than position control — an inherently safer interaction model for fragile objects.

Performance

The gripper was tested on objects ranging from raw eggs and soft fruits to rigid cylinders and irregular 3D-printed parts. Successful grasps were achieved across the full test set without object damage, demonstrating the core advantage of compliance: the gripper adapts to the object rather than demanding the object fit the gripper.
